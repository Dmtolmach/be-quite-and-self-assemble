What is being simulated

Particles and potentials

Each amphiphile is an A–B dumbbell held by a harmonic bond. Every bead feels a Lennard-Jones potential, cut and shifted to zero at rc.

U_LJ(r) = 4ε[(σ/r)¹² − (σ/r)⁶] − U_LJ(r_c) U_bond(r) = ½ k (r − r₀)², k = 200

Only the tail–tail pair (and optionally head–solvent) keeps the attractive well, with rc = 2.5σ. All other pairs are purely repulsive (WCA, rc = 21/6σ). The solvophobic drive is the B–B attraction against an athermal solvent. Number density is 0.6.

NVT dynamics

Equations of motion are integrated with time step Δt = 0.005 in reduced LJ units, with periodic boundaries and cell lists. Two thermostats are available for comparison.

Langevin m dv = F dt − γ m v dt + √(2γ m k_B T) dW Bussi K ← stochastic rescale toward ½ N_f k_B T

Langevin damps momentum locally, so it slows diffusion and removes hydrodynamics as γ grows. Bussi rescaling preserves momentum and leaves the Newtonian dynamics nearly intact, so solvent flow survives. Compare how fast micelles form under each.

What to look for

Start at T* = 2.0: molecules are dispersed. Quench to 0.5: tails find each other and small micelles grow over tens of τ. Heat back to 2.0: micelles fall apart within a few τ. Breakup is fast and assembly is slow, because assembly needs diffusion and collisions.

At fixed T* = 0.5, lower the concentration to about 8%: most molecules stay free. Raise it to 50%: most of them join micelles. Ideal micelle theory says the free-dumbbell density should level off near the critical micelle concentration. The "Free, per σ²" readout lets you test that, but allow 100 τ or more per concentration to equilibrate. Assembly is set by εBB/kBT, so lowering εBB acts like heating.

A micelle here is any cluster of at least 4 molecules whose tails are within 1.5σ. With large heads the clusters stay small (curvature limit). Shrink σA toward 0.9 and the aggregates grow into elongated strips.
